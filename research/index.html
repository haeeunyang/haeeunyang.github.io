  1. Inter-App Dynamics: A VAR Analysis of OTT and SNS Effects on Shopping App Usage
    S. Jung, H. Yang, Y. Cheung, H. J. Yoon, and Y. Kwon
    Journal of Product Research, 2026
    Keywords: inter-app dynamics · media consumption and mobile commerce · OTT effects · VAR model · user heterogeneity
    Abstract DOI

    Research Questions

    • RQ1. Is streaming (OTT) use followed by mobile shopping, as social media use is?
    • RQ2. Do the two differ in how the response unfolds over time?

    Data

    • Hourly app-usage logs from 248 members of a Korean consumer panel.
    • About 31 months from March 2022.

    Method

    • A vector autoregression with 12 hourly lags across six apps.
    • Focus on a shopping app (Coupang), an OTT service (Tving), and a social media app (Instagram).
    • Re-estimated by gender and by age cohort.

    Findings

    • Instagram usage is followed by an oscillating response in shopping-app usage: positive within the first two hours, negative around seven to nine hours, and positive again after that.
    • Tving usage is followed by a steadier response: positive at seven of the twelve lags and never significantly negative. Its cumulative response is about 1.55 times that of Instagram.
    • The responses are larger among women and strongest among users in their 30s and 40s. Users in their 50s and 60s respond with a delay.

    Contribution

    • Shows that streaming, not only social media, is followed by mobile-shopping activity.
    • Shows that the two differ in timing and across user groups, which matters for targeting digital strategies by user characteristics.

    Limitation

    • Usage time is not purchase; the paper does not observe transactions.

    Future research

    • Link app usage to purchase records to see whether the usage response turns into spending.

    This study investigates the inter-app dynamics between mobile application categories, specifically focusing on how media consumption influences mobile shopping behavior. Utilizing hourly usage logs from the Embrain Panel dataset, we applied a Vector Autoregression (VAR) model to empirically analyze the comparative effects of Over-the-Top (OTT) platforms and Social Networking Services (SNS) on shopping app usage over time. The findings reveal distinct temporal patterns: while SNS usage exerts a fluctuating, short-term influence on shopping engagement, OTT usage demonstrates a consistent and cumulative positive impact, suggesting a more sustained pathway to commerce. Furthermore, heterogeneity analyses indicate significant differences by gender and age; the impact of media consumption on shopping is most pronounced among female users and the 30–40 age group, whereas older demographics exhibit delayed responsiveness. By identifying these behavioral disparities, this research highlights the evolving role of streaming platforms as distinct drivers of mobile commerce and provides practical implications for differentiated digital strategies considering user characteristics.

  2. The Impact of Digital Activity on Financial Performance: A Comparative Analysis of the IT and Retail Industries
    Y. Cheung, H. Yang, Y. Kwon, and Y. B. Chang
    Information Systems Review, 2025
    In Korean
    Keywords: Digital Activity · Annual Report · Financial Performance · IT Industry · Retail Industry · COVID-19
    Abstract DOI

    Research Questions

    • RQ1. Is a firm’s digital activity reflected in its market value?
    • RQ2. Does this relationship differ before and after COVID-19, and between the IT and retail industries?

    Hypotheses

    • H1. Digital activity is positively related to financial performance.
    • H2. The relationship differs before and after COVID-19.
    • H3. The relationship differs between the IT and retail industries.

    Data

    • 402 firms listed on KOSPI and KOSDAQ (236 IT, 166 retail), 2016–2023.

    Method

    • Digital activity: the number of digital-related keywords in the business-overview section of each firm’s annual report, lagged one year.
    • Tobin’s Q regressed on this measure, with controls for firm size, profitability, leverage, and liquidity.

    Findings

    • H1 supported: digital activity is positively associated with Tobin’s Q.
    • H2 supported: the association weakened after COVID-19.
    • H3 supported: the association is weaker in retail than in IT. In the retail subsample it is not statistically significant.

    Contribution

    • Measures digital activity from annual-report text.
    • Shows that its link to firm value depends on timing and industry, which argues for industry-specific approaches to digital transformation.

    Limitation

    • Keyword counts are an indirect measure.
    • Firms with stronger performance may invest more in digital activity.

    Future research

    • More direct measures of digital investment.
    • Designs that separate cause from effect.

    The COVID-19 pandemic has significantly accelerated the global shift toward a digital economy, underscoring the critical importance of digital transformation in corporate strategy. This study investigates the relationship between digital activity and financial performance, focusing on the contrasting impacts in the IT and retail industries before and after the pandemic. Using data from 402 firms listed on the KOSPI and KOSDAQ between 2016 and 2023, digital activities were quantified through the frequency of digital-related keywords in annual reports, while financial performance was assessed using Tobin’s Q. The results reveal three key insights: first, digital-oriented strategies positively influence financial performance; second, the strength of this positive relationship weakened in the post-COVID-19 period; and third, the IT industry exhibits a stronger correlation between digital activity and financial performance compared to the retail industry. These findings highlight the strategic value of digital transformation in driving financial success, the necessity of industry-specific approaches, and the ongoing importance of innovation to maintain competitive advantages in a rapidly evolving business landscape.

  1. When Do Strategic Alliances Matter for Firms’ Data Breach Risk? The Role of R&D Alliances
    H. Yang and W. Cho
    Work-in-progress paper accepted for presentation at the Workshop on e-Business (WeB 2026), Lisbon, Portugal, Dec 2026
    Keywords: strategic alliance · R&D alliance · data breach · information security · complexity perspective · transaction cost economics
    Examines whether newly formed strategic alliances are associated with firms’ likelihood of data breaches, and whether R&D alliances moderate this association, using a panel of S&P 500 firms (2006–2025) built from SDC Platinum, Privacy Rights Clearinghouse, and Compustat.
    Abstract

    Research Questions

    • RQ1. Is the number of newly formed non-equity alliances related to subsequent hacking breaches?
    • RQ2. Does this relationship vary with the presence of an R&D alliance?

    Hypotheses

    • H1. Newly formed non-equity alliances are positively associated with subsequent hacking breaches.
    • H2. R&D alliances weaken this association.

    Data

    • S&P 500 firm-years, 2006–2025 (9,852 firm-years, 868 firms).
    • Combines SDC Platinum, Privacy Rights Clearinghouse, and Compustat.

    Method

    • Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) with firm or industry fixed effects.

    Findings

    • H1 not supported: the number of new alliances is not significantly related to hacking breaches.
    • H2 supported: the interaction with R&D alliance is negative and significant, also in the full Compustat sample.

    Contribution

    • Extends research on interfirm relationships and cybersecurity to non-equity alliances across industries and partner types.
    • Shows that the alliance–breach relationship depends on what partners do together, not only on how many alliances a firm forms.

    Limitation

    • The R&D alliance indicator does not measure safeguards directly.
    • Only newly formed alliances are counted, not all active ones.

    Future research

    • Text analysis of 10-K cybersecurity disclosures to measure safeguards before and after R&D alliances.
    • Comparing alliances, joint ventures, and M&As.
    • Testing nonlinear patterns.

    This study empirically investigates the association between a firm’s formation of new non-equity strategic alliances and its subsequent hacking breaches, and whether this association varies by the existence of R&D alliances. In prior research, literature has examined partnerships collectively or focused on specific partner types or industries. To address a gap, this study aims to explain the cybersecurity implications of non-equity strategic alliances across industries, highlighting the differences in the substance of alliance activity. Based on transaction cost economics and a complexity perspective, the study explores why alliance expansion may be associated with cybersecurity risks. It proposes that forming more alliances increases coordination demands regarding information sharing, access rights, and security responsibilities, which can create security gaps. In the case of R&D alliances, inherent appropriation hazards can induce firms to adopt additional safeguards. When these safeguards are implemented in shared systems and procedures, they can mitigate firm-level cybersecurity risks, weakening the positive association between alliance activity and hacking breaches. To test these hypotheses, the study applies Poisson pseudo-maximum likelihood (PPML) estimation on a panel dataset of S&P 500 firm-years from 2006 to 2025, combining data from SDC Platinum, Privacy Rights Clearinghouse, and Compustat. Contrary to the expectation, the results show no significant relationship between newly formed non-equity alliances and hacking breaches. However, the interaction between alliance count and the presence of R&D alliances is negative and statistically significant. These findings suggest that when assessing alliance-related cybersecurity risks, firms may consider looking beyond the number of alliances and understand the content of alliance activities.
